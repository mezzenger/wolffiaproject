## 1. Instructions to Codex

Use this document as the authoritative starting brief for the public website.

Codex should:

1. Inspect the working directory before creating or changing files.
2. Preserve any existing user work and repository history.
3. Build the smallest maintainable static-first implementation that satisfies this brief.
4. Write clear public-facing copy in plain language.
5. Use semantic HTML, responsive layouts, keyboard-friendly navigation, high color contrast, useful alt text, and visible focus states.
6. Keep unverified scientific, nutritional, legal, and food-safety claims clearly marked as drafts or placeholders.
7. Do not publish medical claims, miracle-food language, unsupported nutrition figures, or instructions that could encourage unlawful cultivation.
8. Prefer primary and authoritative sources when later researching facts: government agencies, universities, peer-reviewed papers, extension services, and recognized food-safety authorities.
9. Attribute Wolffia food traditions respectfully. Do not imply that Western researchers discovered its value as food.
10. Keep the public website separate from the private operational Wolffia Tracker.
11. Avoid adding a database, authentication, content-management system, analytics, cookies, or third-party scripts unless the project genuinely requires them.
12. Before declaring the site complete, run the project's build, lint, type-check, link-check, and accessibility checks that are available.

When a fact is not yet verified, Codex should use one of these treatments:

- Add `[VERIFY BEFORE PUBLICATION]` in draft source copy.
- Replace the public claim with cautious wording.
- Omit the claim until a reliable source has been documented.

## 2. Project Identity

### Official working name

**The Wolffia Project**

### Abbreviation

**TWP**

### Primary tagline

**LOCAL FOOD. STRONGER COMMUNITIES. HEALTHIER FUTURES.**

### Secondary tagline

**Small Plant. Big Possibilities.**

Use the primary tagline for the main website identity. The secondary tagline may be used in educational features, social posts, or supporting graphics.

### Thirty-second explanation

> The Wolffia Project is building a small, safe, community-owned method for producing supplemental nutritious food close to where people live. We start with a controlled Wolffia pilot, measure everything, test our assumptions, and turn what works—and what fails—into a guide that churches and community groups can reproduce.

### Core public message

> Building safe, affordable, locally operated food systems—starting with one of the world's smallest flowering plants.

### Mission

Build a safe, low-cost, replicable community system that grows supplemental nutritious food close to where people live.

### Expanded mission

Develop a practical, scalable, community-centered food-production system using Wolffia and complementary crops to strengthen local food security, nutritional resilience, emergency preparedness, education, stewardship, and long-term sustainability.

### Vision

A network of small, locally owned food-resilience sites—hosted by churches, families, schools, nonprofits, or community groups—using documented methods that ordinary people can understand, operate, improve, and reproduce.

## 3. What the Project Is—and Is Not

### The project is

- A careful community food-resilience experiment.
- A controlled Wolffia pilot with documented inputs, conditions, harvests, costs, labor, successes, and failures.
- An education and replication project.
- A way to investigate supplemental food production close to where people live.
- A partnership opportunity for churches, families, schools, nonprofits, researchers, volunteers, and community organizations.
- A platform that may eventually include other lawful complementary crops and food-production methods.

### The project is not

- A claim that Wolffia will replace ordinary agriculture.
- An uncontrolled pond-growing program.
- A medical-treatment or miracle-food campaign.
- A promise of instant food independence.
- A proposal to release water spinach or other regulated plants outdoors.
- A technology showcase whose gadgets outrun the food, safety, or community purpose.
- A project dependent on a PhD, tractor, enormous budget, or lottery win.

## 5. Project Through-Line

The website should consistently connect all work to six verbs:

1. **PROVE** — Operate a small pilot and document growth, inputs, labor, safety, yields, costs, and failures.
2. **FEED** — Develop safe, acceptable ways to use Wolffia as a supplemental ingredient.
3. **TEACH** — Create plain-language guides, demonstrations, and training.
4. **REPLICATE** — Package tested methods so another community can reproduce them.
5. **CONNECT** — Link food production with community partners, lawful complementary crops, recipes, education, and responsible technology.
6. **ENDURE** — Develop governance, funding, legal protections, and leadership continuity.

Wolffia is the signature pilot module, not the entire food system.

### 7.2 Mission

**Purpose:** Explain the reason, values, scope, and long-term community vision.

Include:

- Short and expanded mission statements.
- Vision for locally owned sites.
- The six project goals.
- Why local food resilience matters.

- A clear “What this project is not” section.

### 7.3 Wolffia

**Purpose:** Explain Wolffia accurately without hype.

Include:

- Wolffia is a genus of minute floating flowering plants, often called watermeal; it is not algae.
- Basic visual scale and growth form.
- Long-standing use as food in parts of Southeast Asia, including Thailand, Laos, and Myanmar.
- Examples of traditional use: soups, curries, salads, omelets, and stir-fries.
- Reasons it merits careful study: rapid vegetative growth, shallow contained systems, small footprint, frequent measurable harvests, and potential as a supplemental ingredient.
- A strong explanation that species, water, nutrients, environment, harvest, and processing affect safety and composition.
- A respectful cultural-history section.
- A citations section using primary or peer-reviewed sources.

Do not publish a specific protein percentage, vitamin claim, B12 claim, amino-acid claim, yield, doubling time, or health benefit until it has a documented species-specific source and appropriate context.

Suggested caution box:

> **Fast growth does not automatically mean safe food.** Food-grade cultivation requires correct species identification, clean source stock, controlled water and nutrients, contamination control, documented handling, appropriate testing, and a defined processing method.

### 7.4 Water Spinach

**Purpose:** Preserve the educational and cultural portion of the original concept while clearly addressing regulation and ecological risk.

Include:

- What water spinach (*Ipomoea aquatica*) is.
- Its importance as a vegetable in multiple food cultures.
- Its original role in the broader Wolffia and complementary-crop concept.
- A prominent current legal and ecological caution.

Required position:

> Water spinach remains outside the Virginia pilot unless current authoritative legal review confirms a lawful, permitted, and containment-approved route.

Public material may discuss water spinach educationally. It must not encourage uncontrolled outdoor cultivation, transport, sale, or release into waterways.

Before publication, Codex must verify the current federal, Virginia, and applicable local status through primary government sources. Do not treat an older project note as current legal advice.

### 7.5 Responsible Growing

**Purpose:** Establish trust by making safety and restraint visible.

Include:

- Correct species identification and lawful sourcing.
- Clean source stock.
- Controlled and contained growing units.
- Water and nutrient quality.
- Sanitation and handling.
- Contamination recognition and stop-work rules.
- Harvest and processing controls.
- Testing and traceability.
- Batch records and source records.
- Honest reporting of failures, disposal, and corrective actions.
- Protection of volunteers, host organizations, donors, and recipients.
- No tasting, serving, donation, distribution, or sale until the appropriate safety, legal, labeling, insurance, and liability boundaries have been settled.

This page should explain principles, not provide premature food-production instructions.

### 7.6 Pilot Project

**Purpose:** Show that the project is measurable, limited, practical, and accountable.

Include:

- A contained demonstration rather than a farm-scale promise.
- Possible host settings: church, family, school, nonprofit, or community partner.
- The learning loop:
  1. Define the intended use and safety boundary.
  2. Build a small controlled unit.
  3. Measure inputs and environmental conditions.
  4. Record growth, maintenance, harvest, labor, cost, and failures.
  5. Review evidence and revise the method.
  6. Decide whether to proceed, revise, pause, or stop.
- What will be recorded:
  - Site and growing-unit details.
  - Species, strain, source, and batch lineage.
  - Temperature, pH, conductivity/TDS, dissolved oxygen, and observations where appropriate.
  - Nutrient additions.
  - Harvest and processing records.
  - Contamination and corrective actions.
  - Equipment and maintenance.
  - Labor and accessibility observations.
  - Capital and recurring costs.
  - Experiments and results.
  - Photographs and public-use consent.
- Initial success criteria:
  - No containment breach or unmanaged contamination.
  - Complete records for at least 90% of scheduled observations and tasks.
  - A repeatable operating routine with documented labor and accessibility needs.
  - Measured results that include failures.
  - Clear cost and resource data.
  - A reasoned go, revise, pause, or stop decision.

Suggested line:

> The harvest is food. The bigger harvest is a tested method.

## 9. Accessibility Requirements

The website must be usable by keyboard, screen reader, touch, voice control, and users who magnify text.

Minimum requirements:

- Semantic landmarks and heading order.
- A skip-to-content link.
- Full keyboard navigation.
- Highly visible focus indicators.
- Sufficient text and interface contrast.
- No information conveyed by color alone.
- Responsive text without clipping at 200% zoom.
- Touch targets large enough for comfortable use.
- Form labels and useful error messages.
- Alt text for meaningful images.
- Captions or transcripts for video and audio.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Avoid autoplay, flashing, parallax, and decorative motion that interferes with reading.
- Plain-language summaries before technical material.
- Descriptive link text instead of repeated “click here” links.

Target current WCAG 2.2 AA practices and verify requirements against the current authoritative standard before launch.

## 12. Scientific, Food-Safety, Legal, and Ethical Guardrails

### Scientific claims

- Tie claims to the identified Wolffia species and study conditions.
- Separate dry-weight and fresh-weight measurements.
- Provide context for ranges and variability.
- Avoid extrapolating laboratory results into human health outcomes.
- Do not convert “potential” into “proven.”
- Make uncertainty visible.

### Food safety

- Do not assume that a living culture sold for aquariums is food-grade.
- Do not recommend eating project-grown Wolffia before food-use protocols and testing are approved.
- Address water, nutrient, microbial, contaminant, sanitation, storage, and processing risks.
- Preserve source and batch traceability.
- Publish stop rules and correction procedures when they are ready.

### Legal and regulatory statements

- Verify current rules at the time content is prepared for publication.
- Distinguish federal, Virginia, and local requirements.
- Do not present the website as legal advice.
- Do not encourage obtaining, cultivating, transporting, serving, donating, or selling a regulated organism without documented lawful authority.

### Cultural respect

- Name and credit established food traditions.
- Seek knowledgeable contributors rather than merely rewriting secondary Western summaries.
- Preserve original dish names when reliable spellings and meanings are available.
- Obtain permission for contributed stories, photographs, and recipes.
- Avoid language suggesting that the project invented Wolffia as food.

### Public trust

- Report setbacks and limitations.
- Identify sponsored material and donated equipment.
- Avoid fundraising claims that outrun the evidence.
- Protect volunteers, recipients, and host organizations.
- Use photographs only with appropriate consent.

## 19. Final Editorial Standard

The entire website should embody this project guardrail:

> Wolffia may be useful, but usefulness must be proven under the conditions in which we grow and handle it. We will say what we know, label what we do not know, and stop when safety or evidence says stop.

The site should leave visitors hopeful—but also confident that The Wolffia Project values evidence, safety, cultural respect, accessibility, and community trust more than hype.
