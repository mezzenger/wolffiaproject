Many ways to help

Community ownership starts with honest invitations.

The project needs questions, practical skill, cultural knowledge, careful review, and patience. Participation should fit the person and the safety boundary.

Possible roles

Learn

Ask basic questions, attend an educational session, or help make the language clearer.

Advise

Offer expertise in food safety, horticulture, ecology, accessibility, law, education, or evaluation.

Share

Contribute cultural food knowledge or a recipe with context, credit, and permission.

Volunteer

Help with approved documentation, communications, accessibility review, or contained project tasks.

Host

Discuss a possible setting only after safety, legal, insurance, access, and stewardship questions are addressed.

Partner

Explore an aligned collaboration without assuming endorsement, funding, or public association.

A careful invitation

We are not asking anyone to bet the farm. We are asking trusted partners to help evaluate one contained pilot: a place to learn, document, teach, and decide whether the model deserves a next step.

What participation does not mean

It does not mean permission to grow regulated plants, handle project biomass, use a partner’s name, publish private information, or make claims on behalf of the project. Those boundaries must be explicit.
