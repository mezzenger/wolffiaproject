One contained demonstration

Learn before expanding.

The proposed pilot is a small demonstration—not a farm-scale promise. Its main outputs are safe routines, complete records, honest costs, and a reasoned next decision.

The learning loop

  1. Define the intended use, legal status, and safety boundary.
  2. Design a small controlled unit with a named steward.
  3. Measure inputs and environmental conditions appropriate to the project.
  4. Record growth, maintenance, harvest, labor, cost, accessibility, and failures.
  5. Review evidence with the right technical, food-safety, legal, and community voices.
  6. Decide whether to proceed, revise, pause, or stop.

What a record may include

  • Site and growing-unit details
  • Species, strain, source, and batch lineage
  • Water-quality and environmental observations where appropriate
  • Nutrient additions, maintenance, and equipment
  • Harvest, processing, contamination, and corrective-action records
  • Labor, accessibility, capital, and recurring costs
  • Experiments, results, photographs, and public-use consent

Possible success criteria

The brief proposes criteria such as no containment breach or unmanaged contamination, records for at least 90% of scheduled observations and tasks, a repeatable routine with documented labor and accessibility needs, measured results that include failures, clear cost data, and a reasoned go/revise/pause/stop decision.

These are proposed criteria, not reported achievements. No pilot result is being claimed on this first public version.

Public reporting

Any future public update will share approved summaries rather than private names, exact private locations, raw tracker data, incident details, or unpublished experimental results.

Status

Planned. Host, timeline, public partners, and approved results are not yet confirmed.
