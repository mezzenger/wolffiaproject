Why we are here

A practical food-security question.

Our mission is to explore responsible ways to grow and prepare aquatic foods that may strengthen local food resilience, reduce pressure on land, and help communities think creatively about nutrition and preparedness.

One more tool in the toolbox

This project is not about replacing traditional farming. It is about asking whether carefully managed aquatic food systems could become one useful tool among many: alongside gardens, farms, food pantries, markets, cooking knowledge, and neighborhood relationships.

Our commitments

  • Food security: keep reliable, accessible food at the center of the conversation.
  • Responsible experimentation: start small, contain the system, measure what matters, and stop when safety or evidence says stop.
  • Community education: make the questions understandable to families, educators, growers, and partners.
  • Cultural respect: credit food traditions and welcome knowledge without flattening or claiming ownership.
  • Local resilience: study what works under actual local conditions, not only in ideal examples elsewhere.
  • Measurable learning: document labor, inputs, cost, accessibility, failures, and decisions—not just successes.

What this project is not

It is not a miracle-food campaign, a promise of guaranteed yields, a substitute for public-health advice, or an invitation to grow regulated plants without permission. It is a learning project with visible boundaries.

The long-term vision is modest and ambitious at the same time: locally understood methods, honest records, respectful partnerships, and enough evidence to make a good next decision.

Project through-line

We will say what we know, label what we do not know, and revise the plan when the evidence changes.
