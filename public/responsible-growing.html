Safety before scale

Containment is part of the crop.

Responsible growing means defining the boundary before the first experiment, keeping the system controlled, and making it easy to pause when conditions are uncertain.

This page describes principles, not a production manual. No one should eat, serve, donate, sell, transport, or release project biomass based on this draft.

Our minimum principles

  • Identify the species and source stock before work begins.
  • Use lawful sourcing and confirm relevant permits or restrictions.
  • Keep growing units controlled, contained, labeled, and separated from natural waterways.
  • Track water, nutrients, sanitation, equipment, maintenance, and observations.
  • Define contamination indicators and stop-work rules in advance.
  • Separate food-use decisions from non-food experiments or unapproved material.
  • Protect volunteers, recipients, host organizations, and neighbors from avoidable risk.
  • Record failures, disposal, corrective actions, and uncertainty with the same care as successes.

A visible stop rule

When identification, containment, water quality, sanitation, legal status, testing, or handling is uncertain, work pauses. A pause is not a failure of the mission; it is evidence that the boundary is doing its job.

Food use is a separate decision

Growing something in a controlled vessel does not make it food. Any future food-use pathway would need appropriate species confirmation, inputs and handling controls, testing, processing, labeling, legal review, insurance, and an approved decision-maker. Until then, this site does not invite tasting or distribution.

Trust test

Could a neighbor understand what is happening, what is contained, what is measured, and what would make us stop?
